\en-abstract{
	\lr{
		The increasing prevalence of mental disorders highlights the need for accurate, reliable, and explainable tools that support the detection process. Large language models can generate diagnostic labels together with textual explanations; however, factual hallucinations and the lack of explicit knowledge grounding limit their use in this sensitive domain. This study reproduces, adapts, and compares graph-based methods for explainable mental health detection. The selected methods represent retrieval-based, agent-based, and constrained reasoning paradigms, allowing the role of domain knowledge graphs in supporting reasoning and reducing unsupported claims to be examined. Experiments will be conducted on selected tasks from the IMHI dataset. The methods will be compared in terms of prediction accuracy, explanation quality and faithfulness, response time, and token cost. The generated explanations will also be analyzed at the claim and structural-relation levels using the GraphEval framework.
	}
}
